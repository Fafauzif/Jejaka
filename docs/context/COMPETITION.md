## Jejaka's current competition proposition

Jejaka is a browser-based historical experience. Its first chapter follows a fictional journalist through a reconstructed Jembatan Merah–Kali Mas district in Surabaya during November 1945. Visitors meet fictional residents, record observations in a notebook, make a few local decisions, and experience a restricted view of escalating danger. A separate afterword distinguishes fictional encounters from historical context.

The mechanism is concrete: a recognizable place gives the visitor spatial orientation; named encounters give the place human concerns; notebook choices require attention to what was actually observed; the afterword makes the evidence boundary visible. The current prototype demonstrates that interaction pattern. It does not demonstrate an improvement in learning outcomes or establish that immersive delivery is superior to a well-designed text, exhibit, or film.

Cultural relevance comes from the river, streets, architecture, community concerns, language, and the meanings visitors attach to a historical place. Culture should remain part of ordinary life in the scene rather than appear only as scenery for combat. The journalist's death is an authored dramatic ending, not a historical claim or the educational mechanism by itself.

Its design, participants, consent, comparison condition, and measures would need to be prepared before collecting data. No such pilot has been completed or authorized merely by building this prototype.
