# Cultural source and fiction record — cloth, skill, continuity

Production record, 30 October 2026. This is a claim boundary, not evidence of visitor learning or an assertion that the completed game already communicates every claim below.

## The small cultural focus

Batik is a craft involving wax-resist pattern making on cloth, with knowledge transmitted between people. A finished cloth can carry personal memory; preserving the person, their relationships and opportunities to teach matters alongside preserving the object. Jejaka uses this narrow focus within one fictional civilian encounter. It does not claim that batik is Surabaya's only cultural practice, that all local residents shared one tradition, or that a single rescued cloth saves an entire culture.

## Period and historical boundaries

The batik technique and intergenerational framing have credible pre-1945 context, but this record does not establish a particular practice at Jembatan Merah in November 1945. Use neither a newly named “Surabaya batik motif” nor a claimed district workshop without additional evidence. Sari, Damar, their relatives, shop, words and exact actions remain fiction. A modern UNESCO listing belongs in the optional source view, never the characters' 1945 speech.

The war source ledger remains `HISTORICAL_NOTES.md`. Do not infer a documented attack, exact day, weapon, aggressor's position, destroyed landmark or recovered journalist notebook from this cultural record.

## Review boundary

An AI critic can inspect whether the craft, person, action and outcome are legible in captures and executed sessions. That is a content-legibility review. It cannot establish what a human visitor learned. Human timed play, retention, cultural interpretation and community review remain separate future evidence requirements. Review scores and findings belong outside ordinary player UI.
