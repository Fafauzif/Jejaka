# Jejaka editorial and production standard

## Writing standard

Write each line for the person who encounters it: a visitor in the street-level experience, a reader of the notebook, or a developer maintaining the route. Keep player-facing language separate from technical documentation.

Use concrete observations and short, natural dialogue. Give each speaker a practical concern: finishing a parcel, finding a relative, checking a road, or holding a notebook. Let the visitor infer emotion from those actions. Avoid speeches that explain the theme, ornamental tragedy, invented slogans, repeated rhetorical contrasts, and generic statements about resilience or the human spirit.

Raka writes what he sees, attributes what someone tells him, and marks uncertainty. Do not let his notebook describe an attacker, location, motive, or outcome he could not know. His sentences can become shorter after the injury, but they must remain readable. Do not use garbled spelling to simulate distress.

Use Indonesian as the primary spoken language and English as a faithful subtitle translation. Translate the strength of a claim as well as its meaning. Preserve names, numbers, uncertainty, and the distinction between witness account and historical context. Plain contemporary Indonesian supports comprehension; no claim is made that it reconstructs the exact speech of every 1945 resident.

## Presentation standard

Use restrained typography and spacing. The notebook should resemble an object Raka could carry without sacrificing text legibility. Darken or blur the background behind essential text rather than add decorative panels everywhere. Keep clear keyboard focus, sufficiently large controls, and a visible way to close each overlay.

Keep captions readable over bright walls, dust, smoke, and fire. Identify speakers. Provide captions for sounds that carry story information. A visitor using muted audio or reduced motion must still understand what happened and how to continue.

Avoid points, star ratings, achievement badges, morality meters, and “mission failed” language. The interface guides a historical fiction experience; it does not rank a visitor’s response to civilian suffering.

## Evidence standard

Use real source titles, institutions, dates, and links. Cite a source only for what it supports. Do not use an upload name, a local path, a tool identifier, or a model render as a historical citation. Keep map-derived geometry, reconstructed architecture, fictional encounters, generated production assets, and archival sources distinguishable.

Use “Planning assumption” for a production estimate such as duration. Use “Fictional encounter” for authored dialogue and choices. Do not present either label as evidence of historical truth. Do not claim museum adoption, measured learning impact, voice authenticity, completed visitor testing, or compatibility that has not been checked.

## Review and revision procedure

Evaluate the actual running experience and the packaged source. A written intention does not prove a feature works. A screenshot does not prove a route completes, and a successful export does not prove an animation looks natural.

Use up to five distinct review rounds. In each round:

1. Compare the result with the accepted scope: guided first-person story, notebook-led choices, the researched district, Indonesian dialogue with English support, non-graphic fixed death ending, and an extractable repository package.
2. Inspect the experience as a visitor. Check orientation, movement, legibility, controls, pauses, choices, and the ending. Ask an independent reviewer when available; distinguish that review from the implementer’s own pass.
3. Assess task fulfilment, historical/technical correctness, completeness, clarity/usability, and visual/audio execution. Keep numerical scores and critique exchanges in internal review records, not in player-facing content or the finished story documents.
4. Verify checkable behavior: load assets, follow the route, select each branch, reopen the notebook, pause/resume, restart, mute audio, use reduced motion, inspect console errors, and check source links where accessible. Record untested hardware and browsers as unverified.
5. Fix blocking failures before narrative or visual polish. Recheck affected states so a repair does not erase choices, duplicate notes, replay impacts unexpectedly, or leave stale effects after restart.
6. Stop revising only when no known critical failure or explicit requirement remains unmet and the remaining subjective concerns have no concrete beneficial correction within scope. Do not inflate a score to end the process.

A final handoff should state material limitations briefly outside the experience. Keep private review debates, process commentary, and unsupported claims of perfection out of the package’s public-facing presentation.

## Narrative checks

- Does the visitor understand why Raka has a notebook before the first choice?
- Does Sari have a specific concern beyond providing exposition?
- Does Damar’s guidance remain fallible and local?
- Can a muted visitor identify the disruption and the change in safety?
- Does each branch leave a distinct, persistent notebook entry?
- Does the return revisit a place the visitor recognizes?
- Does Raka’s injury occur independently of a supposed moral score?
- Does the last camera position remain physically his?
- Does the afterword distinguish invented people and events from historical context?
- Can a new visitor restart without inheriting another visitor’s decisions?
