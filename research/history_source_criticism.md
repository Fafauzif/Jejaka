# Source criticism and conflict register

## 1. Film metadata is not a map

H003 is a catalogue description, not a frame-by-frame visual inspection undertaken in this history task. Its locational qualifications and the uncertainty recorded in HC008–HC009 must be preserved. A capture of an image through a model’s vision interface would still need comparison with source metadata.

Required procedure before using a frame as a landmark reference:

1. Identify its film and shot/frame locator.
2. Inspect the visible skyline, road direction, riverbank, neighbouring buildings and any legible sign.
3. Test the proposed location against period cartography.
4. Separate the catalogue identification from the modeller’s own identification.
5. Preserve all uncertain matches in the asset brief.

Never use soundtrack detail as evidence from a silent source. New sound effects or environmental audio belong to the production layer.

## 2. Contemporary reports are attributed observations

H002 contains both diary and dispatch material. Their purposes differ. Treat an author’s claimed observation as a source statement and retain the document heading. Do not copy period slurs or propaganda terms into neutral product text. Do not turn reported losses or claimed battlefield success into calibrated simulation parameters.

The digitised text contains OCR defects and the item order is not strictly chronological. If a critical number, place name or date is unclear, verify against the page image. Page numbers in this kit are one-based PDF pages, not invented printed foliation.

## 3. Ultimatum dating remains qualified

HC006 and HC007 do not prove that one source is simply wrong. An order, a letter, a dropped leaflet, delivery to an official and the compliance deadline can have different dates. The object in H004 is explicitly described as a replica in its title. The root research pass inspected the full municipal page, which associates9Novemberwith leaflet distribution; a later subagent retrieval timed out. That does not establish the issue date of every related order or correspondence.

For the initial environment, none of this needs to become visible geometry. For an episode featuring the ultimatum, inspect a legible document reproduction and write separate issue/distribution/deadline fields before locking the narrative.

## 4. Mallaby date and mechanism

HC005 and HC015 conflict. The modern municipal heritage book’s caption must not overwrite the event date established in the dedicated museum account. A photograph’s creation date and a death date are different metadata fields. The conflict is retained instead of silently repaired.

The precise killing mechanism and individual responsibility remain outside the reconstruction’s supported claims. If the event is ever included, present the contested reconstruction as such and require a separate focused evidence review.

## 5. Old illustrations and modern photographs

H011 combines sources of widely different dates. Its illustrated nineteenth-century residency and bridge views can supply discovery leads. They do not automatically show surviving 1945 geometry. Its modern views are helpful for orientation only until the change history is established.

The book’s chapter on Jembatan Merah is printed pp152–158, corresponding to PDF pages163–169. The page label printed inside the document is authoritative when citing the book; record both systems if handing an image to the agent.

An especially important exclusion: an old residency building visible in an early photograph must not be rebuilt in 1945 solely because the modern district is historically associated with that administrative centre. Establish its survival or demolition date independently.

## 6. Scholarly photograph interpretation

H012 offers a useful method of comparing visible content with contextual documents (HC016). It also makes proposed location interpretations. A scholarly proposal is not an architectural survey. Follow its image identifiers to the holding institution, inspect the original and test the location against the selected map.

An article’s Creative Commons notice does not necessarily transfer the rights to third-party archive photographs reproduced inside it. Use the holding institution’s terms for the original image.

## 7. Citation and access discipline

- Original archival object inspected: may support what is actually visible or readable.
- Catalogue inspected: supports metadata and discovery, not unseen geometry.
- Full text with selected sections inspected: cite only inspected sections.
- Abstract or bibliographic record: a lead for retrieval, not verification of the whole work.
- Failed retrieval: preserve failure status, do not write a confident summary from memory.
- Duplicate publications using one original: one line of corroboration, not multiple independent witnesses.

The source register records these statuses so a later compaction cannot promote a discovery lead into a verified reference.
