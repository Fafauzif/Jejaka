# Period environment and prop research rules

This document specifies how to avoid unsupported visual detail. It is an original production protocol, not a claim that every listed prop has been documented at the selected location.

## Buildings and streets

Prioritise the relationships that determine the district’s appearance: water edge, bridge approaches, street intersections, block depth, building alignment and major roof silhouettes. Detail should follow evidence density. Small ornaments are lower priority than a correct building mass and frontage orientation.

A photograph can support visible storey count and façade rhythm. It cannot by itself prove a rear courtyard, concealed roof construction or exact wall thickness. Perspective measurement requires a reliable scale reference and camera assumptions. Record approximate dimensions as approximate.

Keep these attributes separate:

- Footprint observed on a period plan.
- Height observed or inferred from an image.
- Façade visible in a dated photograph.
- Materials inferred from architectural type.
- Texture colour selected by the art director.
- Damage state documented for a specific time.

Do not colourise a monochrome photograph and treat the output as recovered paint evidence. Choose restrained provisional colours, document them and keep materials editable.

## Water, banks and crossings

Do not infer historical bank position from modern concrete embankments without checking period plans. Record quay edges, stairs, retaining walls and landing areas only where shown. Avoid raising urban streets into dramatic hills for cinematic effect. Artistic relief exaggeration, if ever used, must be separately labelled and switchable.

Water surface treatment is a presentation choice unless environmental evidence supports a specific state. Reflections, colour, floating matter and boats must not imply documentary certainty.

## Signs, flags and writing

The chronological urban observations in HC003 show why flags and road obstacles deserve research, not arbitrary placement. For a hero sign or slogan, obtain the original legible image, transcribe the text, record its date and identify its location. Use the actual text or leave the sign neutral. Avoid invented patriotic quotations presented as historical graffiti.

Keep sign textures in separate files. Never depend on image-generation lettering for a verified quotation. Modern logos, fonts, road markings, advertising and tourism signage are excluded unless the build is explicitly a present-day comparison layer.

## Military objects

The base scene does not require a complete army. Include military equipment only after choosing a dated scenario and confirming the subtype. Record whether a vehicle was operational, parked, damaged or captured in the evidence. A captured vehicle’s origin is not necessarily its operator at the photographed moment.

Do not use a generic modern military asset pack merely because it is convenient. Recognisable helmets, rifles, uniforms, trucks and tanks are strong date cues. Leave unverified equipment out of the default scene instead of filling it from model memory.

## Civilian life

Avoid making every person an armed combatant. Clothing, footwear, headwear, transport and shop goods need references matched to place and date. A single photo depicts a particular moment and camera selection, not a population sample. Avoid assigning a person an ethnicity, allegiance or occupation from appearance alone.

For optional atmosphere, use a separately identified reconstructed layer. Dense crowds and dramatic mass evacuations should never be added to a street merely to make the scene feel wartime.

## Damage and debris

Keep intact architecture and damage in separate collections. Date every source of blast damage, burnt façades, blocked routes or destroyed vehicles. Scattered rubble is not a substitute for a building-specific damage reference.

If damage is visually important but not sufficiently documented, provide a comparison mode or exclude it from the historically supported base. Do not simulate physics and call the resulting debris the historical event.

## Weather, light and sound

Start with neutral daylight that exposes geometry for review. Dramatic fog, rain, smoke and golden-hour lighting can obscure mistakes. Treat each as an artistic layer unless a source establishes its occurrence at the selected time and place.

Environmental audio would be newly authored sound design. A silent archive film cannot establish the sounds heard in its location.

## Anachronism audit before delivery

1. Inspect every modern-reference object for later alterations.
2. Search the asset manifest for future dates and missing date fields.
3. Hide all later memorial layers.
4. Remove modern street furniture, road signs, traffic lights and vehicle silhouettes unless individually supported.
5. Check unit insignia and flags if human or military assets have been added.
6. Verify no old photograph has been treated as November 1945 solely because it shows the same place.
7. Review each exact-looking feature with no source: label its reconstruction status or simplify it.

Report unresolved uncertainties in the handoff. An attractive render is not an accuracy certificate.
